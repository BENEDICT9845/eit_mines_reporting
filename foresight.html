Market foresight canvas · mining water, 2026–2036

Mining needs more water every year, and nobody can see where it goes.

One trend, what it does to the market, the future we want, and the steps to get there. Industry-wide, not one company.

1Current trend & 10-year development

Trend: copper mines use more water each year (+18% vs +13% more output, 2015–19), while only about 1 in 5 reports water per mine.

Water used by copper mines: 2.95 km³ in 2019 (WU Wien model), growing 4.3% a year if the 2015–19 trend holds. About 1.5× by 2036.

Drivers

BusinessCopper demand from grids, EVs and data centres; ~30% supply gap by 2035 (IEA).
PhysicalFalling ore grades: more water per tonne of metal. 66% of mining sites face high water risk (ICMM).
RegulationEU battery due diligence, ESRS water reporting, Critical Raw Materials Act.
TechnologySatellites + machine learning estimate water at mines that don't report.
SocietalTowns and farms compete for the same water in drought (Cadia, 2019).

Observed & expected milestones

  1. 2019Only 97 of 507 copper mines report their water
  2. Jul 2026Industry dataset of ~12,000 sites maps risk, not water use
  3. 2027EU battery passport (Feb) and due diligence (Aug); revised ESRS
  4. 2030EU raw-materials targets: more mining and processing in Europe
  5. 2034Two-thirds of Chile's mining water from the sea
  6. 2035Copper ~30% short of demand

2Market consequences

If the trend unfolds as expected

Water risk hidden in company totals: one number for many mines.
Buyers and banks price risk from estimates they can't check.
Mines in dry basins lose permits and community support first.
Every new rule means another manual report and questionnaire.
Copper stays outside EU battery checks and stays unmeasured.

3Desired future

Different from the "nothing changes" path

Every mine reports water in, out and reused, in one format that adds up.
Water is compared between mines the way carbon is today.
Water terms in supply contracts and loans reward careful mines.
Models fill only real gaps, with an honest uncertainty flag.
Mines and communities share one water account per river basin.
